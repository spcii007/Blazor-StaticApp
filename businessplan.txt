AI-Accelerated .NET Modernization — Business Plan
Oct 1, 2026 
Executive summary
The business sells one outcome: legacy Microsoft-stack apps become modern, documented, AI-ready systems that the client's own team can maintain with AI. Year 1 target revenue is about $350K as a solo consultant, with a conservative floor near $150K.
Positioning line: "I modernize legacy .NET apps with AI in half the time of a traditional rewrite, then teach your team to keep them modern."
Three core offers sit inside one client journey:
Codebase archaeology recovers what the app does before anyone touches it.
AI-accelerated modernization moves it to current .NET, module by module, behind a test safety net.
Agent-ready integration exposes the modernized app to AI agents through clean APIs and MCP servers.
A paid assessment is the front door. Team training and monthly retainers turn one-off projects into recurring revenue. A YouTube series that modernizes a real legacy app on camera is the main credibility and lead engine.
All prices below are US-market estimates for a senior independent consultant (effective rate $150–200/hour). Validate them with your first 3–5 sales conversations and adjust.
Target market and ideal client
The ideal client is a mid-sized company (50–2,000 employees) running a business-critical in-house app on .NET Framework, with a small dev team and no appetite for a risky full rewrite.

Offer 1: AI-accelerated legacy .NET modernization
Fixed-price migration of a legacy .NET app to current .NET, priced by size and complexity, always preceded by a paid assessment. AI agents do the bulk conversion; you own architecture, verification and sign-off.
Typical scope: .NET Framework to modern .NET (LTS), WebForms or MVC 5 to Blazor or ASP.NET Core, WCF to REST or gRPC, EF6 to EF Core, on-prem to Azure, plus CI/CD.
Method: assessment ? characterization tests ? convert one module (pilot) ? convert the rest in sprints ? parallel run ? cutover.

Offer 2: Codebase archaeology and knowledge recovery
A fixed-price engagement that turns an undocumented system into documentation, tests and AI context files the team can use immediately. It is the easiest first sale: low risk, no production changes, useful even if they never modernize.
Deliverables:
Architecture overview and diagrams (system context, components, data flows)
Business rules catalog: every rule found in code, with file location and plain-English description
Data dictionary for the main database
Characterization tests on the 5–10 most critical workflows
AI context files (for example CLAUDE.md, copilot-instructions.md) so any AI coding tool understands the codebase
"Bus factor" report: which areas only one person understands, and the risk

Offer 3: Agent-ready legacy apps
A secure API and MCP (Model Context Protocol) layer that lets AI assistants such as Microsoft Copilot, Claude or in-house agents read from and act on the client's app, without a rewrite. It turns "we have old software" into "our software works with AI."
Typical use cases: a plant supervisor asks an assistant for today's open quality holds; a fundraiser asks for lapsed major donors in a region; a support agent's AI drafts a reply using live order data.

Sales and marketing beyond YouTube
Your first 3–5 clients will almost certainly come from your existing network, not YouTube. Spend about 60% of marketing time on direct outreach until month 6.
Warm network (weeks 1–8): list 100 former colleagues, managers and clients across fundraising, manufacturing, telecom and automotive. Send a personal note offering a free 30-minute "legacy app health check" call. Expect 10–15 conversations and 2–3 paid assessments.
Founding-client offer: the first 2 assessments at $5,000 in exchange for a case study and testimonial.
LinkedIn: 3 posts a week: one lesson from the build, one client-style story, one short video clip. Rename your headline to the positioning line.
Referral partners: managed IT service providers, Microsoft Dynamics and Azure partners, Blackbaud consultants and ERP resellers. They meet legacy apps daily and rarely modernize custom code. Offer 10% of the first project.
Speaking: local .NET user groups, .NET Conf community events, nonprofit tech events (such as NTEN) and regional manufacturing associations. One talk a quarter.
Written case studies: every project becomes a one-page case study with hours, cost and outcome.
Website: a one-page site with the three offers, prices "starting at", the YouTube series and a booking link.
Sales process: discovery call ? short written proposal within 48 hours ? paid assessment ? fixed-price migration quote ? training add-on ? retainer proposal in the final project week.

Risks, mitigations and business setup
The two risks that sink most solo consultancies are a fixed-price project that overruns and an empty pipeline after the first big project; the plan is built around both.
Risk
Mitigation
Fixed-price overrun
Paid assessment first; price per phase; written change control; 15% contingency in every quote
Client code sent to AI tools
Enterprise AI plans that do not train on customer data, or tools on the client's own accounts; data-handling clause in the MSA
AI-generated bugs or license issues
Characterization tests before conversion, human review of every change, dependency license scan
Pipeline gaps (feast or famine)
Retainers by month 6; keep 1 day a week for marketing even when busy
YouTube grows slowly
Treat it as a sales asset you send to prospects, not as the lead source; outreach carries months 1–6
AI tools change quickly
Sell a method (assess, test, convert, verify, train), not a specific tool
Solo capacity ceiling
Line up 1–2 trusted .NET subcontractors by month 6
